About

I never got taught this. I went and got it.

I study geology. Nobody handed me a computer science degree, a bootcamp or a mentor — I started with an empty folder and a browser tab, and I kept going because the feedback loop was the most addictive thing I had ever found.

Nine repositories later I run Nara, a white-label platform that meal-subscription businesses use to handle their clients, menus, deliveries and payroll. I designed it, built the frontend, and shipped it to people who complain to me directly when something breaks. That is the best teacher I have found.

I work with AI every day — Claude Code sits in my terminal the way an IDE sits on my desktop. It has not made me lazy; it has made me faster at the part I actually care about, which is deciding what should exist and how it should feel.

I want to build a company. I have started. Now I want to get very good, next to people who already are.

Theming
The hard part

One codebase, many identities — and dense operator screens that non-technical kitchen owners read at 6am. Trilingual too: Cyrillic and Latin have different rhythm, so a layout tuned for one breaks in the other.

What it taught me

Theming is an architecture problem, not a CSS problem. Once tokens flow from tenant config instead of a stylesheet, a new brand takes minutes instead of a fork.

Journey

How I got here

Four years, compressed. The interesting part is the gap between the first two entries.

  1. 2022

    Started university

    Geology, English sector, Baku State University. Not computer science — that part I had to go get myself.

  2. 2024

    First lines of real code

    Stopped watching tutorials and started shipping badly. Everything I know traces back to that switch.

  3. Early 2026

    Nine practice builds

    Cafés, tour operators, meal delivery, a doner shop — not client work, just me picking real-world businesses and building the site each one deserved. Every single one finished and deployed, because half-done teaches you nothing.

  4. Mid 2026

    Founded Nara

    Turned a repeated problem into a product: white-label software for meal-subscription businesses. Designed it, built it, shipped it.

  5. 2026

    Nara in production — then Bullur

    Real businesses, real customers, real money moving through interfaces I built. Then I started the next one, and built far less before showing it to anyone.

  6. Next

    Learn from people better than me

    I have taken this as far as one person can. The next jump is a professional team, a mentor, and a codebase bigger than mine.

Why I build

Because a good interface is the closest thing to telepathy we have.

I like the moment it becomes real

There is a specific second when a thing stops being a layout and starts being something a stranger uses without thinking. I build for that second.

Interfaces are an argument

Every screen is telling someone what matters. Good ones make the right thing feel obvious. That is design, not decoration.

I want to build a company

Not someday — I already started. Nara is the first attempt. I want to get very good at this alongside people who already are.
